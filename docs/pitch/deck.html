The problem

Banks pick the product first,
then look for someone to sell it to.

Wrong product

Offers go to groups, not people

A saver gets a loan offer. A student gets a premium card. A customer is offered something they already have.

Wrong moment

Timed by campaigns, not by life

Offers arrive when a campaign runs — not when salary lands, a goal is set or spending changes.

No reason given

So customers ignore them

Nobody explains why an offer fits. Customers tune out, trust drops and good products go unused.

The clues are already in the account: salary dates, regular bills, money left over each month, and goals.

03

What makes it different

Five things a normal offer engine doesn't do.

  • It can say no. When no product would really help, it suggests nothing — so when it does speak, people listen.
  • It reads real Nigerian statements. NIP, POS, WEB and ATM lines become income, bills and habits — nothing typed in by hand.
  • It explains everything. Why it fits, why now, what it used, and why the other products weren't picked.
  • It waits for the right moment. When salary lands it takes a fresh look — and messages at most once a week.
  • It never invents terms. Only Zenith's published facts, each with its source. Everything else: “subject to Zenith Bank's current requirements”.

How it decides

Five steps. Every one visible to the customer.

01

Understand

Income, spending, money left over and habits — only from what's allowed.

02

Find the need

Goals and patterns become needs, each with a strength score.

03

Match

Every Zenith product is scored. Published conditions are checked.

04

Decide

Only a score of 80+ is shown. Limits stop repeat offers.

05

Explain

Why it fits, why now, and why not the others.

Score = needs 30% · goal 20% · habits 15% · can-you-get-it 15% · timing 10% · preferences 10%. Clear rules, not a black box — a bank's risk team can check every weight.
